# Laser vs Inkjet Printing on Oddy A4 Adhesive Sheets

## Comparative Media Analysis: Laser vs Inkjet

Printing adhesive labels requires understanding the physical chemistry of the substrate and printing mechanism.

### Laser Printing (Toner Heat Fusion)
- **Mechanism:** Laser printers use an electrostatic drum and high-temperature fuser rollers (170°C – 210°C) to melt plastic toner powder into the paper fibers.
- **Advantages:** Completely waterproof, smudge-proof, impervious to alcohol/oil, high contrast crisp barcodes.
- **Risks:** The extreme fuser heat can soften cheap adhesives. Oddy A4100 series uses heat-stable acrylic adhesive designed to resist up to 220°C without oozing.
- **Driver Setting:** Always set Media Type to **"Labels"** or **"Heavy Paper (120–160gsm)"**. This slows the paper transport motor, allowing adequate thermal transfer time.

### Inkjet Printing (Dye & Pigment Inks)
- **Mechanism:** Piezoelectric or thermal nozzles spray liquid ink droplets directly onto the paper surface.
- **Advantages:** Zero heat exposure prevents any curl or adhesive softening. Excellent for photographic full-color labels.
- **Risks:** Standard dye inks will smudge if exposed to water droplets or condensation (e.g. refrigerated bottles). Use pigment-based black ink or cold-laminate after printing for moisture resistance.
- **Driver Setting:** Set Paper Type to **"Matte Coated Paper"** or **"Plain Paper High Quality"**.
